N04|Trap number one: the hidden markup. When the dealer "finds you financing," the bank approves you at one rate, called the buy rate. The dealer can write your contract at a higher rate and keep part of the difference. On our example, just two and a half extra points means about twenty-five hundred dollars more in interest. You'll never see the bank's real rate unless you ask for it. And it adds up: in late twenty twenty-five, the average dealer was making almost two thousand dollars per car in that office alone. Not on the car. On the paperwork.
N05|Trap number two: the extras. GAP coverage. Extended warranties. Credit insurance. Sometimes useful, often overpriced, and usually rolled into your loan, so you pay interest on them too. In twenty twenty-three, the CFPB fined Toyota's lending arm sixty million dollars. Regulators said it pushed these extras, which added seven hundred to twenty-five hundred dollars to the average loan, and then made them hard to cancel. If you saw our last video, you know the pattern: easy to buy, hard to cancel.
N06|Trap number three: "Take it home today." It's called spot delivery. You sign, you drive home, you show your family. Then, days later, the phone rings: "Your financing fell through. Come back and sign a new contract." At a higher rate. Consumer advocates call it yo-yo financing. People who re-sign end up paying, on average, about five percentage points more. Warning signs: blank spaces in the contract, a form that says the sale is "conditional," or leaving without a final contract at all.
N07|Trap number four: "We'll pay off your old car." If you owe more than your trade-in is worth, they don't make that debt disappear. They roll it into your new loan. N09|Okay. Enough bad news. Here's your playbook. Five moves that put the power back on your side of the desk. One: get pre-approved before you walk in. Talk to a bank or a local credit union first, and ask if they work with newcomers. Walk in with a rate in your pocket, and the dealer has to beat it. Two: ask one question: "What's the buy rate?" If they won't answer, that tells you everything. Three: negotiate the out-the-door price, not the monthly payment. A low payment can hide a longer loan and more interest. Four: ask for every extra in writing, line by line, and say no to anything you didn't ask for. Five: never drive away with blank spaces or a "conditional" contract. If the financing isn't final, the deal isn't final.
