## COLD OPEN (0:00–0:30)

**[ON SCREEN: two identical silver sedans side by side. Text: SAME CAR. SAME PRICE.]**

Two people walk into the same dealership. Same car. Same sticker price. They even shake the same hand.

**[Text slams in: ONE PAYS $16,000+ MORE]**

One of them drives home paying over sixteen thousand dollars more. Not because of the car. Because of what happened *after* the price was agreed.

Dealers make a big chunk of their money in a small office you barely notice: the finance office. Today I'm walking you through seven moves they use there, with real numbers, and the exact sentence to say back to each one.

Number seven is the one almost nobody catches.

---

## TRICK 1: "What monthly payment are you looking for?" (0:30–1:35)

**[ON SCREEN: salesperson smiling, notepad. Text: "WHAT PAYMENT WORKS FOR YOU?"]**

It sounds friendly. It's a trap.

The second you give a monthly number, the dealer can hit it in many ways. The easiest is just stretching the loan.

**[STAT CARD]**
Same $40,000 loan, same 6.35% rate:
- 60 months: **$780/mo**, about **$6,790** in interest
- 84 months: **$591/mo**, about **$9,650** in interest

*Source: WorthWired calculation · rate = Experian avg. new-car APR, Q2 2026*

The payment drops almost $190. Feels like a win. But you just agreed to pay about **$2,860 more** in interest, and you're stuck in that loan two extra years.

The average new-car loan is already almost **70 months** long, according to Experian. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's advice is simple: judge a loan by its *total cost*, not its monthly payment.

**[SAY THIS card]**
> "I'm not shopping by monthly payment. What's the out-the-door price?"

---

## TRICK 2: "We'll pay off your old car" (1:35–2:45)

**[ON SCREEN: banner "WE'LL PAY OFF YOUR TRADE. NO MATTER WHAT YOU OWE!"]**

You've seen the ads. Here's what "we'll pay it off" usually means.

If you owe more than your car is worth, that's called negative equity. The dealer pays off your old loan, then adds what's left over on top of your *new* loan.

**[STAT CARD]**
- **29.6%** of new-car trade-ins were underwater in Q2 2026
- Average amount underwater: **$6,884**

*Source: Edmunds, Q2 2026*

Let's roll about $7,000 of old debt into our $40,000 example:
- Without it: **$670/mo**
- With it: **$790/mo**, and about **$8,650 more** paid over the loan

*Source: WorthWired calculation*

They didn't pay off your old car. You did. With interest. And you start the new loan even deeper underwater.

Edmunds found buyers who rolled in negative equity are paying a record **$944 a month** on average.

**[SAY THIS card]**
> "How much negative equity is in this deal, and where exactly does it show up in the contract?"

---

## TRICK 3: The interest rate you never see (2:45–3:50)

**[ON SCREEN: two numbers on a glass wall. Small one: "BUY RATE". Big one: "YOUR RATE".]**

When the dealer "finds you financing," they send your application to lenders. The lender approves you at a rate, called the **buy rate**.

The dealer can then offer you a **higher** rate and keep part of the difference. It's called **dealer markup** or **dealer reserve**, and the CFPB has been publishing warnings about it since 2013.

**[STAT CARD]**
Typical markup reported in the industry: **0.5 to 1.5 percentage points**
A 1.5-point markup on our $40,000, 72-month loan:
- **+$28/month**, about **$2,080** extra over the loan

*Sources: CFPB 2013 auto lending bulletin · industry reporting · WorthWired calculation*

Twenty-eight dollars a month sounds like nothing. That's exactly why it works.

**[SAY THIS card]**
> "I'm already pre-approved at ___%. Can you beat it?"

Walk in with a pre-approval from your bank or a credit union. Now *their* rate has to compete with yours.

---

## TRICK 4: The extras in the final paperwork (3:50–4:55)

**[ON SCREEN: contract with highlighted lines: GAP · Extended warranty · Paint protection · Tire & wheel · VIN etching]**

You've agreed on a price. You're tired. Then the finance office slides over the "final" paperwork, and it has extra products in it.

Some of these can be useful for some people. **All of them are optional.** But when they're rolled into the loan, you pay interest on them too.

**[STAT CARD]**
Add $3,000 of add-ons to our example loan:
- **+$50/month**, about **$3,600** total paid for $3,000 of products

*Source: WorthWired calculation*

In 2024, the FTC finalized a rule, the CARS Rule, that would have forced dealers to clearly say add-ons are optional and get your written consent. In January 2025, a federal appeals court **vacated it** on procedural grounds. So right now there's no federal rule doing that for you. Some states are writing their own.

**[SAY THIS card]**
> "Please remove every optional product and reprint the contract. I'll decide on each one separately."

---

## TRICK 5: "Take it home today" (4:55–6:00)

**[ON SCREEN: keys dangling in front of the camera. Text: "TAKE IT HOME TONIGHT!"]**

Driving off the lot the same day feels great. But sometimes your financing isn't actually finished.

It's called **spot delivery**. If the loan doesn't get approved on the terms you signed, some dealers call days later: *"Your financing fell through. Come back and sign a new contract."* Usually at a higher rate.

When it's used to pressure you into a worse deal, consumer advocates call it **yo-yo financing**.

**[ON SCREEN: contract close-up, highlighted: "Subject to financing approval"]**

Look for language like *"subject to financing approval."* If it's there, the deal isn't final.

Rules vary by state, but generally, if the financing truly falls through, you should get your down payment and your trade-in back, not a new contract you're pressured to sign.

**[SAY THIS card]**
> "Is my financing final and approved, or is this conditional? I'll take the car when it's final."

If it happens to you, you can file a complaint with your state attorney general.

---

## TRICK 6: The shell game (6:00–6:55)

**[ON SCREEN: a 4-box worksheet: PRICE · TRADE-IN · DOWN PAYMENT · MONTHLY PAYMENT. Arrows moving money between boxes.]**

Many dealers negotiate four things at once: the price, your trade-in, your down payment and your monthly payment.

Why? Because when everything moves together, they can give you a "great" trade-in value and quietly take it back in the price or the rate. You see one number improve and you never see where the money went.

The CFPB lists these as **separate** things you can negotiate: the price, the down payment, the interest rate, the loan length and the trade-in value.

**[SAY THIS card]**
> "Let's agree on the out-the-door price first. Then we'll talk trade-in. Then financing."

One number at a time. In that order.

---

## TRICK 7: Your credit score, before you ever arrive (6:55–7:50)

**[ON SCREEN: credit score gauge sliding from red to green]**

This is the one almost nobody catches, because it happens before you even walk in.

**[STAT CARD]**
Average new-car APR, Q1 2026:
- Super-prime credit (781+): **4.55%**
- Poor credit: **16.01%**

Same $40,000, 72 months:
- **$636/mo** vs **$868/mo**
- Interest: about **$5,800** vs **$22,500**

*Sources: Experian, Q1 2026 · WorthWired calculation*

That's about **$16,700** more for the same car. And dealers know who walks in without checking their credit first, because those buyers don't know what rate they *should* get.

We broke this down in our "Same Car, $14,000 More" video. Link on screen.

**[SAY THIS card]**
> Before you shop: pull your credit, fix errors, and get a pre-approval. Your rate should be decided *before* you sit down.

---

## THE PLAYBOOK (7:50–8:25)

**[ON SCREEN: checklist animating in]**

So here's the playbook:
1. **Get pre-approved** before you shop.
2. Negotiate the **out-the-door price**, never the monthly payment.
3. Ask: **"What's the buy rate?"**
4. **Remove every add-on** and decide on each one separately.
5. Don't take the car until the **financing is final**.

Screenshot that. It can save you thousands.
